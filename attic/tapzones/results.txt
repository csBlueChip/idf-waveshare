Tap zones - experiment results (0.7.50 .. 0.7.57, 2026-11-20). Shelved:
too unstable to be useful.

Question: can the IMU tell WHERE on the screen a tap landed? Idea: a tap
off-centre twists the board; the gyro swing direction (gx/a, gy/a, gz/a)
should point at the zone. Board flat on a table unless noted; taps with
the finger pad (nail = more ringing, worse).

Accuracy = leave-one-out on the training taps (each tap classified
against centres built from all the others), nearest centre.

Findings
  - Top vs bottom (ROT 0) separates well with peaks: gx ~200 dps for the
    top row, ~40 for the bottom (2x2: 100 %).
  - Left vs right is unstable: gy / gz of the right-hand zones flip sign
    from tap to tap (the board rings; the largest sample is either lobe).
    Some taps flip all three signs. One centre per zone averages the
    lobes to ~0, so those taps land in the neighbour.
  - The chip's tap engine re-reports the rebound; the 300 ms look-back
    then re-found the same spike = duplicate training taps (0.7.57: a
    spike is used once; untested in the test phase).
  - Weak taps (< ~1.8 g) are noise: imu.tap.min.
  - First swing (mean over imu.tap.win from the spike's onset, baseline
    removed) helped rows a little, hurt columns.
  - Results change with how the board is held / rests, tap strength and
    nail vs pad: a calibration does not carry over well.

Untried ideas
  - k-NN on all training taps (store every tap, ~2.3 KB for 16 x 12)
    instead of one centre per zone; or two centres per zone (both lobes).
  - Sign-invariant features (|g|) plus the sign of the dominant axis only.
  - Accel shape (ax ay az around the spike) as extra features.
  - Higher rate / longer window; the full waveform (template matching).
  - Rows only (top / bottom) as a reliable 2-zone feature.
